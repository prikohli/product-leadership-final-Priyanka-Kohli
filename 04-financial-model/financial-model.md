## Lab notes

**Make your evaluation and funding decision**

- **What assumption is doing the most work? If this number is 20-30% off, what changes?:** The upsell-rate improvement from 6% to 8% is doing the most work. That 2-point increase creates all of the incremental Enterprise revenue. If the lift is 20-30% lower, the gain falls to roughly 7.4%-7.6% instead of 8%, cutting the expected incremental Enterprise conversions by 20-30%. The 20% time-to-quote reduction matters only if it actually produces the upsell increase.
- **What is the structural problem in this case? Look past the headline numbers for something that does not hold up on closer inspection.:** The model never proves the link between 20% faster quoting and an increase from 6% to 8% upsell. Those are different metrics. We are given a productivity improvement, but the business case is funded by a conversion assumption. Also, without the Standard customer base size, the $28,000 Enterprise ACV, 12% churn, and $620 CAC cannot be translated into actual ROI.
- **Is the kill criterion complete and actionable? Does it name the consequence, or hand the decision back to the room?:** No. There is no explicit kill criterion. The case states targets (20% faster quotes, upsell from 6% to 8%) but does not say: “If upsell remains below X% after two quarters, stop investment.” Without a threshold and consequence, the decision is handed back to the room.
- **Your verdict: FUND / FUND WITH ONE CONDITION / DO NOT FUND. If a condition, name it; otherwise explain in one sentence.:** FUND WITH ONE CONDITION. Condition: prove within one quarter that the AI estimator increases Standard-to-Enterprise conversion above the current 6% baseline and is on a credible path to 8% within two quarters. Otherwise the business case rests on an unvalidated assumption rather than the measured 20% productivity gain.
